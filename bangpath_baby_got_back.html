01 // OVERVIEW THE BIG PICTURE
// Mission Brief

The original Baby Got Back is built on a single bass synth ostinato in C minor. The harmonic palette is extremely sparse: Cm is home, with brief Eb (D#) and Db (C#) movements — these are the 3rd and 2nd of the C minor scale, creating a descending chromatic motion. The song is structurally a rap track — the "chorus" is really just the hook over the same groove. There are no verse/chorus harmonic shifts in the guitar sense. Everything is Cm.

For rhythm guitar: you are playing the role of the synth bass. Your two main options are (A) hold the Cm barre chord with a locked rhythmic stab pattern throughout, or (B) play the actual bass ostinato notes as a riff on the lower strings. Both are documented. Choose based on your band's arrangement — if your bassist covers the ostinato faithfully, Option A (chord stabs) is correct. If you want the guitar to carry the riff, Option B works. Don't do both simultaneously.

// The Shape Advantage

All three chords (Cm, Db, Eb) use the identical Am-barre shape. The only difference is position: Cm=3fr, Db=4fr, Eb=6fr. You learned one shape. You're done. The passing chord movements are just 1-fret and 3-fret slides of the exact same hand position. If you can play Cm cleanly, you can play all three.

05 // ARRANGEMENT OPTIONS CHOOSE ONE — DON'T MIX
A
// CHORD STAB — RECOMMENDED
Hold Cm barre throughout. Play a locked rhythmic stab pattern — downstrokes with palm muting between hits. Let your bassist play the ostinato riff. You provide rhythmic weight and harmonic foundation. When the hook hits Eb/Db, briefly shift the barre shape up. Clean, heavy, appropriate for a metal arrangement. This is what a rhythm guitarist does on a rap track — you're the engine, not the melody.

Best when: Your bassist is covering the ostinato riff faithfully.
B
// OSTINATO RIFF — ALTERNATE
Play the bass ostinato as a guitar riff on the low E string: e|—8—8—8—11—9—9—11—9—| at 120 BPM, 8th notes. This is the recognizable hook. It's one string, 3 fret positions (8, 9, 11), no barre required. The riff IS the song — when people hear it, they'll recognize it immediately. More technically demanding at tempo but more melodically interesting.

Best when: You want the guitar to carry the recognizable riff, or your bassist is playing something different.
// ⚠ Don't Do Both Simultaneously

If guitar plays the ostinato riff (Option B) AND bassist plays the ostinato riff, you get muddy doubling at the same pitch. If guitar plays chord stabs (Option A) AND bassist plays a different groove, you lose the riff entirely. Coordinate with your bassist before rehearsal. The chart below documents Option A (chord stabs) as primary, with Option B riff positions noted.

09 // TECHNIQUE NOTES
01
// Cm BARRE ENDURANCE
Baby Got Back is approximately 4 minutes long. You will hold the Cm barre chord at fret 3 for almost the entire duration, playing a stab pattern at 120 BPM. This is a hand endurance test disguised as a comedy song. Build to this — run the song at 90 BPM first, increasing to 120. If your hand tightens, you're gripping too hard. The Cm barre should be firm enough to ring cleanly, not strangled. Check your thumb position: it should be behind the middle finger of your fretting hand, not creeping over the neck.
02
// PALM MUTING FOR HIP-HOP FEEL
The hip-hop stab feel comes from controlled palm muting between downstrokes. Rest the heel of your picking hand lightly on the strings near the bridge — enough to slightly deaden the sustain without fully killing the note. When you strum down, the palm briefly lifts; when you hit the muted chick on the &, the palm re-engages. This "on-off" palm is what creates the staccato hip-hop pocket. Without it, you just have a strummed chord. With it, you have a rhythmic weapon.
03
// THE EB/DB SLIDE TRANSITION
Cm (fret 3) → Eb (fret 6) → Db (fret 4) → Cm (fret 3) is all the same barre shape. Practice this specific sequence in isolation: stab at fret 3, slide to fret 6 (same pressure, no need to re-form the chord), stab at fret 6, slide to fret 4, stab, slide back to fret 3. The slides happen during the muted chick beats — your hand moves while the muted stroke covers the transition. This is the only technical challenge in the song. Everything else is endurance.
04
// OPTION B: THE RIFF (LOW E STRING)
If playing Option B (the ostinato riff): frets 8-8-8-11-9-9-11-9 on the low E string, 8th notes at 120 BPM. Use downstrokes throughout — this is a rap/metal riff, not a lead line. The pattern sits in three positions: fret 8 (C), fret 9 (Db), fret 11 (Eb). Index covers fret 8/9, ring covers fret 11. The jump from fret 9 to fret 11 is two frets — a stretch that becomes automatic quickly. Run this riff before adding any other guitar technique. It must be locked before it lives in a performance.
