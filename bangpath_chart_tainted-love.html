// Barre Reality Check

All four main chords in this song are barre chords. None of them are open. This is why Tainted Love is the Phase 2 graduation test — if your barre chords aren't reliable, this song will expose it immediately at tempo. 02 //
THE PATTERN
REPLACING THE SYNTH PULSE

The Soft Cell original runs on a relentless, mechanical synth arpeggio pulse. Your job in the metal arrangement is to replace that pulse with a rhythm guitar that has the same mechanical inevitability — but with physical weight. The pattern is syncopated stabs with tight palm muting between hits. It should feel like a machine, not a strummer.

Contrast is the technique: The chorus works because the verse was tight and muted. Don't undercut that contrast by playing the chorus too carefully. OUTRO / FADE
Repeat to end · Original fades · !bang!path! does NOT
Pattern Bbm – Db – Eb – Csus2 repeat
!bang!path! arrangement decision: The Soft Cell original fades out. In a live metal arrangement you do not fade — you build and end hard. Discuss with the band how you want to close it: a final chord with a clean cutoff, a drum fill and stop, or a big held Bbm with a pick scrape. Whatever you decide, the ending needs to be rehearsed as deliberately as everything else. A song that doesn't know how it ends will always feel unfinished.
05 //
TECHNIQUE NOTES
METAL-SPECIFIC EXECUTION
TECH 01 //
THE MUTED STAB — Your Primary Tool

The motion: Barre the chord fully. Bring the pick down with full contact across all strings. Immediately — within the same motion — drop the fretting hand pressure to lightly touch the strings without depressing them. The chord rings for roughly a 16th note then dies. The pick never leaves the string zone.

What good sounds like: A short, percussive "chunk" with a clear pitch. Not a ring, not a buzz, not a thud. The chord is audible but brief. The space after it is silent, not noisy. If you hear string noise between stabs, your palm isn't making contact during the rest.

Drill: Set metronome to 80 BPM. Play one Bbm stab on beat 1 only. Let bar pass. Stab on beat 1 again. Focus entirely on how cleanly the chord dies after each hit. The mute is as important as the attack.

TECH 02 //
RAPID BARRE TRANSITIONS

The problem: At 144 BPM with two chord changes per bar, you have roughly 0.2 seconds between barre chord positions. This is the main technical challenge of the song. Sloppy transitions are audible because the muted stab format leaves no room to hide.

The solution: Keep your index finger curved and firm. Move the entire fretting hand as a unit — don't unspool individual fingers and re-plant them. Practice the Bbm → Eb → Csus2 triangle as a separate drill. These three chords cover most of the song.

// Transition drill — 60 BPM, one change per beat Bbm (fret 1) → Eb (fret 6) → Csus2 (fret 3) → Bbm // Count "1-2-3-4" and change on each number // Increase BPM by 4 when you can do 2 clean minutes

What good sounds like: No audible gap between chords. The muted dead space that the pattern requires should be intentional — not because you haven't landed the next chord yet.

TECH 03 //
CHORUS PICKUP — PATTERN SWITCH

The moment: The last bar of the pre-chorus ends on Db. You then need to switch from the tight stab pattern to open eighth notes as the chorus hits on Eb. This transition needs to feel instantaneous — there should be no "adjustment period" where the chorus starts loosely.

Practice this specifically: Run bars 5–6 of the pre-chorus (Bbm → Db), then launch the chorus Eb with the open drive pattern. The switch in feel is the hardest moment in the song. It needs its own drill time.

// On the Tempo Jump

144 BPM is fast. The barre chord transitions at full tempo are genuinely difficult, especially the Eb (fret 6) → Csus2 (fret 3) move. Do not rush the ladder. Being clean at 108 BPM is more useful than being sloppy at 144. The band will set the final tempo in rehearsal — it may land somewhere between 130–144 depending on how the arrangement breathes with drums. Your job is to be clean at whatever tempo they lock in.

// What Good Execution Sounds Like
The verse should sound like machinery. Every stab is identical in length, attack, and dynamics. The space between stabs is not empty — it's occupied by palm muting contact that makes a faint percussive hiss. The chorus unlocks and sounds like the same energy released from a cage. The pre-chorus builds tension by moving through unfamiliar chord territory (G, Cm) before landing the chorus payoff. If someone listening can't clearly hear the difference between the verse and chorus energy, the stab pattern isn't tight enough. The mechanical verse makes the open chorus matter.
SONG GATE — TAINTED LOVE
  • All four main barre chords ring cleanly 8/10 on first attempt, no repositioning
  • Stab pattern locked at 100 BPM with metronome — no rushing between chords
  • Eb → Csus2 transition clean at tempo with no audible gap
