01 //
THE PROGRAM
WHAT !BANG!PATH! ACTUALLY IS
// Mission Statement

You have bass experience. You understand rhythm. You have the gear. You have a setlist that matters to you. What you don't have is a strumming hand that functions as a rhythm instrument, barre chords that work under pressure, or a practice framework that stops you from grinding the same comfortable exercises into meaninglessness. !bang!path! exists to solve exactly those three problems, in exactly that order, before touching a single song.

// The Problem With Enthusiasm
Enthusiasm
Is Not Evidence

Unstructured practice produces marginal improvement and significant frustration. Feeling like you're progressing and actually progressing are not the same measurement. This program replaces subjective feel-good with binary gate tests that cannot be argued with. Either 8 out of 10 barre chord attempts ring clean. Or they don't.

// The Output
STAGE
READY

Full setlist. At tempo. With a band. In front of humans. That is the only success condition this program recognizes. Everything else — the exercises, the gates, the phases — exists in service of that single, non-negotiable, entirely achievable outcome.

01
Gate System Is Mandatory
No phase advancement without passing all exit criteria. "Close enough" is not a gate status. "I'm almost there" is not a gate status. Open or closed.
02
5 Hours/Week Minimum
Ten hours recommended in Phases 1 and 2. Practice volume below the floor for two consecutive weeks triggers a formal diagnostic review — not forgiveness.
03
Clean Tone Only
High gain is forbidden for all chord work. Distortion masks bad technique. The Epiphone into the NUX on clean, at low volume, is where the actual work happens.
04
Honest Self-Assessment
The program produces zero useful output if scores are inflated. You are the only one who loses when you lie to the gate test.
02 //
THE GATE SYSTEM
WHY BINARY MATTERS
// Design Rationale
The Calendar Does Not Control Advancement

Some students close all five Phase 1 gates in six weeks. Some take ten. The program does not care either way. What it prevents is the most common failure mode in self-directed learning: completing a phase while remaining incompetent at its content. Gates are binary because graded progress is how people trick themselves. A gate at 80% is a gate that doesn't exist — it just gives you permission to fail in the next phase at higher cost.

Every gate has a specific, reproducible test. "Sounds clean" is not a gate. "8 out of 10 individual string plucks ring without buzz or mute" is a gate. The difference is that the latter cannot be argued with — not by you, not by anyone.
