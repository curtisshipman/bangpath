// Overview

You have a bass background, musical literacy, and the right gear. You also have open chords that don't ring cleanly under pressure, a strumming hand that doesn't know it's a rhythm instrument yet, and barre chords that are aspirational at best. Phase 1 fixes all of that. Not "good enough" — automatic.

This is eight weeks of unsexy, foundational work. Your Kiesel and your PRS are sitting there looking at you judgmentally. The metronome is going to tell you things you don't want to hear. Listen to it anyway.

⚠ Ground Rules
The exit criteria are not suggestions. You don't move to Phase 2 because you hit week 8. You move when you pass the gates. Some people hit them in 6 weeks. Some take 10. The program doesn't care about your feelings — only your accuracy. Track your results honestly.

// Phase 1 Exit Gates

ALL FIVE MUST BE PASSED. NO PARTIAL CREDIT.

  • ▸ Open chord transitions E → A → D → G → C (any order) at 80 BPM — no hesitation, rings clean, 2 minutes continuous
  • ▸ Down-up strumming pattern held steady for 2 minutes at 80 BPM without drift or collapse
  • ▸ A-shape barre chord rings cleanly — 8 out of 10 attempts (every string audible, no buzz, no mute)
  • ▸ F-shape (E-shape) barre chord rings cleanly — 6 out of 10 attempts
  • ▸ B string rings clearly on both barre chord shapes — 7 out of 10 attempts isolated test

// Gear Setup for Phase 1

Use the NUX Mighty for most practice — you don't need the Katana's volume during exercises. Keep gain low or at zero for chord work. Distortion hides bad technique. You want to hear every dead note.

Settings Recommendation
Clean channel, light crunch at most. If a chord sounds good with high gain, it might still be sloppy. Clean tone is your truth serum. Save the Katana crunch for strumming pattern work in weeks 5–8 where you want to feel the energy.

Guitar selection: The Epiphone LP or Revstar for barre chord work — their necks will build real hand strength. The PRS Vela is easier; save it for when you're testing your gate performance, not building the technique. The Kiesel is your reward for passing gates.

Barre Chords

These use moveable shapes. Learn the shape, then move it up and down the neck. The fret number is your root position.

A-Shape Barre
e |--X or bar--| B |---fret+2---| G |---fret+2---| D |---fret+2---| A |---ROOT fret-| E |---X---------|
Index bars A string at root fret. Fingers 2,3,4 fret D,G,B two frets up. Or use ring finger mini-barre on D,G,B.
F-Shape Barre (E-shape)
e |---full bar---| B |---full bar---| G |---full bar---| D |---full bar---| A |---full bar---| E |---ROOT fret--|
Index full-bars all 6 strings at fret. Middle=A str+1, Ring=D str+2, Pinky=G str+2. This is the beast.
On the F-Shape
Your B string is failing because your index finger is bending away from the fret. The fix: rotate your index finger slightly so the bony side (not the pad) contacts the B string. Your finger has a natural arch — you are fighting gravity and anatomy when you use the pad. Stop. The bony edge is flat. Use it.

Barre Chord Root Note Logic

The A-shape: your root note is on the A string. The F-shape: your root is on the low E string. That's all you need to know to move chords around the neck. Memorize the notes on those two strings over time — for now, here are the ones you'll actually use:

A string frets (A-shape root): Fret 2 = B Fret 3 = C Fret 5 = D Fret 7 = E Fret 8 = F Fret 9 = F# Fret 10 = G Low E string frets (F-shape root): Fret 1 = F Fret 2 = F# Fret 3 = G Fret 5 = A Fret 7 = B Fret 8 = C Fret 9 = C# Fret 10 = D

// Core Exercise Library

These exercises are the building blocks. Each week's schedule references them by name. Learn them now so the weekly schedule reads cleanly.

Chord Transition Exercises

EX-1: The One-Minute Changes

Purpose: Build chord switching speed through volume of reps, not thought.

How: Set metronome to 50 BPM. Pick two chords. Switch on every beat — not every bar, every beat. One chord per click. Count how many clean switches you make in 60 seconds. Write it down. Compete with your previous number.

Start with: E → A. Then A → D. Then D → G. Then G → C. Then C → E. Then randomize.

Advancement: When you hit 30+ clean switches per minute at 50 BPM, bump to 60 BPM. Gate is 30 clean at 60 BPM before moving on.

In a Big Country — open chord cavalry charge, E/A/D transitions everywhere
The Whole of the Moon — G and C will be your workhorses
EX-2: Chord Hold + Lift

Purpose: Fix dead notes by training all fingers to land simultaneously, not sequentially.

How: Form a chord slowly. Lift ALL fingers about 1cm off the strings, keeping the shape. Land them all at once. Strum. If any string buzzes or mutes, lift and retry. No metronome — this is about precision, not speed. 5–10 minutes.

Targets: G Major (finger spread), C Major (finger stack), F-shape barre (full commitment).

EX-3: Pivot Finger Transitions

Purpose: Find the finger that doesn't need to move between chords and anchor it.

How: Identify the shared note/position between chord pairs. Keep that finger planted, move only the fingers that need to change. This cuts transition time in half.

Key pivots:

  • E → Am: index stays on 1st fret F note... wait, E to Am has no pivot. This is the hard one. Just rep it.
  • C → Am: fingers 2 and 3 stay. Only index moves.
  • G → Em: fingers 3 and 4 stay on high strings (4-finger G voicing) — both chord shapes share those notes.
  • A → D: no direct pivot, but D string stays open. Use it as a reference.
EX-4: Spider Crawl (Fretting Hand Independence)

Purpose: Build finger independence and calluses simultaneously.

How: Chromatic crawl, one finger per fret. Frets 5–8 on all strings, ascending and descending. Not musical. Purely mechanical. Use with metronome at 60 BPM, one note per click. Keep all fingers hovering close to strings.

Duration: 5 minutes max. This is warm-up, not the main event.

Strumming Hand Exercises

Bass Player Translation
Your bass brain tracks the beat from the downstroke. In strumming, you need to internalize that both the downstroke AND upstroke carry rhythmic weight. The upstroke is the "and." You already know "1 and 2 and 3 and 4 and" — you just haven't trained your right arm to live inside that grid. That's the gap. These exercises close it.
EX-5: Air Strum First

Purpose: Decouple the strumming pattern from chord anxiety. Train the arm alone.

How: Set metronome to 60 BPM. Do not touch the guitar. Strum the air in front of you. Down on every beat. Feel the arm move from the elbow, wrist loose. After 2 minutes, add upstrokes on the "and." Another 2 minutes. Your arm should feel like a pendulum — down-up is one continuous motion, not two separate gestures.

Key insight: The arm never fully stops. Even when you skip a strum, the arm keeps moving. You just miss the strings intentionally.

EX-6: The Open E Drone

Purpose: Anchor the strumming hand without chord-change cognitive load.

How: Mute all strings with your fretting hand (lightly rest fingers across strings, don't press). Strum freely. Set metronome to 60 BPM. Play: D D D D (all downstrokes) for 2 minutes. Then D-U D-U D-U D-U for 2 minutes. Then D-D-U-D-U for 2 minutes (the "money strum"). No chord changes. Just the arm. Track drift.

This Corrosion — Sisters of Mercy: relentless 8th note strumming is the core engine of this song
Shout — Tears for Fears: driving down-up pulse
EX-7: The Money Strum Pattern

Purpose: The single most useful strumming pattern in rhythm guitar for this genre.

Pattern (1 bar of 4/4): Beat: 1 + 2 + 3 + 4 + Strum: D D U U D U ↓ ↓ ↑ ↑ ↓ ↑ Read as: Down, [miss], Down-Up, [miss]-Up, Down-Up

How: Learn it at 50 BPM on muted strings. When it's automatic, add one open chord. When that's clean, add chord changes. The pattern comes first — always.

Tainted Love — this pattern variant drives the 8th-note grid of the original
True Faith — New Order: mechanically regular strumming is intentional
EX-8: Metronome Displacement Drill

Purpose: Steel your internal pulse against metronome anxiety. If you tighten up near a click, you're following it. You should be generating the beat — the metronome just checks you.

How: Set to 60 BPM. Play 4 bars of steady down-strokes on an open chord. Then turn off the metronome mid-pattern. Keep going for 8 bars. Turn it back on. Did you drift? This is your honest score.

Barre Chord Exercises

EX-9: A-Shape Approach — Fret 5 First

Purpose: Build the A-shape barre on the easiest fret before moving toward the dreaded low positions.

How: Start your A-shape barre at fret 5 (D major barre). The tension is lower here. Press, strum all strings slowly, pluck each string individually. Identify dead notes. Adjust. Move to fret 3 (C barre) when fret 5 is clean. Move to fret 2 (B barre) when fret 3 is clean. Week 5 target: fret 2 clean on demand.

Ring finger mini-barre option: For the A-shape, some players barre the D, G, and B strings with the ring finger instead of using three separate fingers. Try both. Use whatever rings cleanest for you — consistency beats orthodoxy.

Last Night I Dreamt — The Smiths: barre chord shapes are central to Johnny Marr's approach
Broken Wings — Mr. Mister: A-shape barre chord progressions throughout
EX-10: The B-String Isolation Test

Purpose: The B string is the tell. If it buzzes or mutes, your index finger is arching. This test makes it impossible to hide.

How: Form your barre chord. Pluck ONLY the B string. Does it ring clearly? Adjust finger position micro-incrementally — roll slightly toward the bony ridge of your index finger. Pluck again. Repeat. Do this 10 times. Record your success rate. Gate: 7 out of 10.

Why it's hard: The B string sits in the natural arch of your index finger. It requires active compensation — slight inward rotation of the index, thumb positioned directly behind the middle finger on the back of the neck, no death grip.

EX-11: Barre Chord Pump

Purpose: Build the hand strength needed to hold barre chords without fatigue collapse.

How: Form A-shape barre at fret 5. Hold for 5 seconds. Release completely (open your hand, let the blood flow). Re-form. Strum. Release. Repeat 10 times. This is strength training, not speed training. Do NOT push through pain. Stop if your forearm cramps badly.

Callus note: This and EX-9 will accelerate callus development on your index finger's side edge — exactly where you need it.

EX-12: Barre to Open Transitions

Purpose: The real setlist challenge is moving between barre chords AND open chords in the same progression.

How: Open E → A-shape barre at fret 5 (D). Back to open E. Slow. 40 BPM, one chord per bar. Increase speed when each transition is clean. Also: open A → F-shape barre at fret 3 (G barre) → open D.

Under the Milky Way — The Church: mix of open and barre chords in progression

F-Shape Index Finger Technique — Read This
Three adjustments that actually work:
1. Move your index finger closer to the fret wire — not on top of it, but very close. Distance = pressure required = buzz.
2. Rotate slightly toward the bony side. Feel the difference between your index pad and the ridge toward the thumb side. The ridge is flatter. Use it.
3. Drop your elbow away from your body. This angles your wrist so the index can press more flat. Try it right now before continuing.

// Troubleshooting: When It's Broken

Chords Buzzing Randomly

Root cause: fingers not pressing close enough to the fret wire, or adjacent finger touching the string above it. Check: press each string individually after forming the chord. Identify the specific string. Then look at which finger is causing it — it may not be the finger fretting that string.

Transitions Breaking at Speed

Root cause: you're thinking about where the fingers go instead of having them memorized physically. Solution: slow down to where it's clean. Not "slightly slower" — dramatically slower. Half speed. Build up again. Speed is the result of accuracy, not the training method.

Strumming Arm Tensing Up

Root cause: you're gripping the pick too hard. The pick should feel almost loose. Tension travels up the arm. Shake your arm out, consciously loosen your grip, and re-approach the exercise. Most strumming problems are tension problems.

Barre Chord Fatigue Collapse

Root cause: death grip. You don't need as much pressure as you think — you need precise pressure in the right place. Try pressing with 60% of your maximum force. If it buzzes, adjust position before increasing force. Strength builds over weeks; technique is immediate.

Progress Stalling in Week 5–6

This is the normal plateau. The brain has automated the easy patterns and is now fighting to hold them under increased demand. It's not regression — it's consolidation. Keep the sessions shorter and more focused. Drop to one exercise per session if needed. Quality beats quantity here.

// Final Note

Phase 1 is boring. That's the point. The things that become automatic aren't the ones you practiced excitedly — they're the ones you practiced boringly and consistently until they stopped requiring attention.

Your bass background is an asset for pulse and subdivision. It's not an asset for your fretting hand, your strumming arm, or your calluses. Those you're building from scratch regardless of prior history. The metronome doesn't care what you played in college. Neither do the exit gates.
