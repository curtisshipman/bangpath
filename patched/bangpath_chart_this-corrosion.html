POSITION SET OPENER #01
// The Opener — What This Song Has To Do

This Corrosion opens the set. That means it has one job before any notes are played: it has to tell the audience immediately what kind of night this is. The original is a gothic cathedral of a song — 11 minutes of escalating grandeur with a choir, an orchestra, and Andrew Eldritch somewhere in the middle of all of it. Your version is a four-piece with an attitude problem.

The good news: the chord structure is simple. Three chords in the verse (Dm · G · F), three different chords in the chorus (G · F · C). The pre-chorus is where it gets complicated. The job is to make simple chords sound enormous — that's a dynamics and conviction problem, not a technique problem.

// Live Arrangement Decision — Required Before Rehearsal

The original is nearly 11 minutes. You need to decide with your band what the live arrangement length is before you build the chart into muscle memory. Recommended options: a tight 5-minute version (2 verses, 2 choruses, 1 pre-chorus, outro) or a full 7-8 minute version that earns its runtime. Playing the full 11-minute original note-for-note as an opener will kill the set's momentum. Decide on structure first, then practice that version — not the album version.

// F Barre — The Endurance Factor

F at fret 1 is the hardest shape in this song and it appears in every verse and every chorus. The full E-shape barre at the first fret is the tightest barre position on the guitar — your index finger has to work hardest there. In a metal arrangement at higher gain, a buzzing F chord is immediately audible. Practice the Dm → F transition specifically: open chord to full barre, clean every time.

// The A# Problem

A# (Bb) at fret 1 is a barre chord that appears twice in every pre-chorus, squeezed between open chord shapes. The jump from Am (open) to A# (barre fret 1) to G (open) happens quickly. The barre needs to land instantly — no "building up to it." Practice Am → A# → Am as a micro-drill: 60 BPM, one change per beat, until the A# lands on the first try every time.

06 //
TECHNIQUE NOTES
FOUR THINGS TO MASTER
TECH 01 //
THE F BARRE — OPEN THIS SONG

Why it's first: F at fret 1 is in every verse and every chorus. If the F barre is unreliable, this song falls apart. The first fret is the hardest barre position — maximum finger tension, minimum leverage. If your F chord buzzes, the song buzzes.

The fix: Place your index finger as close to the first fret wire as possible without sitting on top of it. Roll your index finger slightly toward the nut side so the bony edge makes contact rather than the flat pad. Apply pressure from the thumb behind the neck directly opposite your index finger. The barre doesn't require brute force — it requires correct mechanics.

Dm → F drill: 60 BPM, one change per bar. Dm (open) → let ring → F (barre fret 1) → let ring. Every string must be audible on the F chord. Practice until the F lands clean 9/10 without adjustment.

TECH 02 //
THE GOTHIC STAB — SILENCE AS TECHNIQUE

What makes this song gothic: The silence between chord stabs. The original Sisters of Mercy recording has massive space between hits — it sounds cavernous, ominous, oppressive. In your metal arrangement, that silence needs to be maintained. If you're strumming busily between the stabs, you're playing a different song.

The mechanics: After each downstroke, the fretting hand releases pressure slightly — strings still contacted but not depressed — while the palm lightly touches the strings near the bridge. The result is immediate muting. The chord should die within a 16th note. Practice: stab Dm, mute in under a 16th, sit in silence for two beats, stab again. The mute should be instantaneous, not gradual.

TECH 03 //
PRE-CHORUS SPEED DRILL — 7 CHORDS CLEAN

The sequence isolated: Dm → Am → A# → Am → G → A# → D

The two problem transitions: Am → A# (open to barre fret 1, must be instant) and A# → G (barre to open, fretting hand has to release cleanly without noise). Both happen twice per sequence.

Drill approach: Start at 60 BPM, one chord per beat. Play each chord as a single clean downstroke. Increase by 4 BPM when you can run the full 7-chord sequence without hesitation or noise. Target is clean at 90 BPM before integrating into the song.

TECH 04 //
VERSE → CHORUS CONTRAST — THE DYNAMIC SWITCH

The two modes: Verse = tight, muted, dark, percussive. Chorus = open, ringing, bright, driving. The switch between them happens at the moment the pre-chorus resolves to D and the G chorus chord hits.

Practice the moment: Run bars 5-6 of the pre-chorus (A# → D), then launch the chorus G with full eighth-note drive. The switch needs to be instantaneous — no "settling in" period. Record yourself doing this transition. If there's an audible hesitation or a change in feel that takes more than a beat to establish, it needs more isolated drill time.

// Suggested Live Arrangement — 5 Minute Version

Intro (6 bars) → Verse 1 (6 lines) → Pre-Ch (×2) → Chorus (×4) → Post-Ch (×2) → Verse 2 (4 lines) → Pre-Ch (×2) → Chorus (×4) → Outro / end hard on G

This cuts Intro 2, Verse 3, and Pre-Ch 3 — all repetitions of material already heard. The song structure and all its emotional moves are preserved. At 100 BPM this runs approximately 5 minutes, which is appropriate for an opener. Discuss with your band and lock the arrangement before rehearsal begins — practice the live version, not the album version.

// What Good Execution Sounds Like
The intro lands like a verdict. Each verse stab is deliberate and heavy, the silences between them absolute. When the pre-chorus moves through its 7-chord sequence it sounds inevitable — like a machine changing gears, not a guitarist scrambling. The chorus explodes out of the pre-chorus resolution with full conviction. G → F → C rings openly and drives forward. The post-chorus pulls the darkness back immediately. The whole song has a gothic cathedral quality — enormous space, deliberate movement, nothing wasted. At no point does it sound hurried or casual. It sounds like the Sisters of Mercy if they had traded the sequencer for a drummer and turned up a Marshall stack.
