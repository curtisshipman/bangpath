At this tempo the strumming pattern becomes a quick, light touch — see Section 05 for technique adaptation. The 75% practice target is 143 BPM.

// ★ THE BIT — STAGING NOTES FOR LIVE PERFORMANCE

This is the joke. A metal band that plays new wave covers ending their set with the Friends theme is the funniest thing you will do all night. The crowd will either lose their minds or stare in confused silence, both of which are correct outcomes.

Staging suggestions: (1) Don't announce it. Just start playing the intro riff. Let the crowd recognize it. (2) Play it completely straight — no winking, no ironic distance. Full commitment to the song is funnier than indicating the joke. (3) The intro lead line is recognizable in 0.5 seconds — that recognition moment is the punchline. (4) If you want to lean in: the vocalist can do a quick "we'll be there for you" dedication to the crowd before launching in.

01 // OVERVIEW THE BIG PICTURE
// Mission Brief

The Rembrandts' I'll Be There For You is a power-pop track with a driving rock pulse — much more guitar-forward than most songs in this set. At 191 BPM it's the fastest song you'll play all night. The original has a memorable lead guitar intro riff (the melody everyone knows) and a tight rhythm guitar underneath running a chuck-mute pattern. For rhythm guitar: your job in the verse is the chuck pattern, not the melody. The chorus opens up to full strummed chords. The bridge has some barre chord movement. The solo section — you hold the rhythm groove while lead solos over the verse chord loop. Don't try to play the intro melody on rhythm guitar.

// ⚠ 191 BPM — Speed Management

191 BPM is fast. The strumming pattern needs to be light and efficient — small strokes from the wrist, not big arm sweeps. The chuck mutes in the verse are your friend: they break up the continuous strumming demand and give micro-rests. At this tempo, over-strumming will kill your wrist in 90 seconds. Think of the pattern as a controlled flick, not a strum. The chorus open chords are actually easier at this tempo because you're just riding the beat.

// CHUCK MUTE TECHNIQUE AT 191 BPM
The "✕" beats are chuck mutes — your fretting hand releases pressure slightly (strings touch frets but don't ring), your picking hand still strums. This produces a percussive "chk" click. Without the chucks it's just fast strumming; with them it's a locked pocket.

08 // TECHNIQUE NOTES
01
// WRIST ECONOMY AT 191 BPM
191 BPM is not joking around. Full arm strumming at this tempo is physically unsustainable. Use wrist rotation — the pick moves from the wrist, not the elbow. Small arc, maximum efficiency. The down-strokes should feel like a flick, not a sweep. If your forearm is burning after 30 seconds, you're strumming too big. Shrink the motion until you can play for 3 minutes without tension building.
02
// CHUCK MUTE EXECUTION
Chuck mutes at 191 BPM need to be split-second: fret the chord, play the beat, then instantly reduce fretting-hand pressure so strings touch frets without ringing — the picking hand still passes through. This produces the "chk" percussive click. Practice the chuck in isolation: form an A chord, strum down, immediately loosen pressure (don't lift!), strum again. The percussive hit is what gives the verse its pocketed feel.
03
// Bm vs F#m — SAME FRET · DIFFERENT SHAPE
Both Bm and F#m live at fret 2 but are completely different shapes. Bm is an Am-shape (5-string barre, fingers 1-1-1-3-4). F#m is an Em-shape (full 6-string barre, fingers 1-1-1-1-3). They appear in the same section (bridge). Drill the shape distinction specifically before running the bridge at speed — the shape needs to be automatic, not thought through.
04
// PATTERN SWITCHING — VERSE TO CHORUS
The verse (Pattern A, chuck mutes) to chorus (Pattern B, open strum) transition needs to be immediate and audible. The pre-chorus E chord is the switching point — as you land on E going into the chorus, drop the chucks and open up the strumming. This dynamic contrast is what makes the chorus feel like a lift. If both patterns sound the same, the arrangement loses its shape. Verse tight, chorus open.
09 // FEEL DESCRIPTION
// THE TARGET STATE
You are closing a metal set with the Friends theme. The correct energy is: completely unironic, total commitment, full volume, played as if this is the most important song you have ever performed in your life. The joke is that you're not joking. If one person in that room starts clapping along by the chorus, you've won. If by the second verse the whole crowd is singing "I'll be there for you" at a metal show — that is a perfect gig. The A chord at the end should feel like a victory lap. Land it, hold it, let it ring. Walk off stage. Never explain it.
