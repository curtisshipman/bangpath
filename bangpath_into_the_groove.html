01 // OVERVIEW THE BIG PICTURE
// Mission Brief

Madonna's Into The Groove is a synth-pop disco machine with a deceptively mechanical pulse. The original has almost no live guitar — this is a keyboard-and-drum-machine track. Your job in the !bang!path! metal arrangement is to replace the synth stabs with rhythmic chord attacks that lock to the groove without turning it into a power-chord thrash fest. The feel is tight, syncopated, almost funk-adjacent. The trap is playing it too heavy. The goal is controlled aggression — think "playing in a box," not "kicking down the door."

// ⚠ Barre Load Warning — Worst in the Set

Every single chord in this song is a barre chord. Cm (fret 3) · Ab (fret 4) · Fm (fret 1) · Bb (fret 1) · Gm (fret 3) — there is no open chord anywhere in this arrangement. At 117 BPM with continuous 8th-note strumming, this will destroy an undertrained fretting hand. Build to tempo gradually. If your hand tightens, stop. This is the final boss of the setlist for a reason.

07 // TECHNIQUE NOTES
01
// THE CM ↔ GM SHAPE TRAP
Cm (fret 3, 5-string Am-shape) and Gm (fret 3, 6-string Em-shape) are at the same fret but completely different shapes. In the verse, you switch between them constantly. Drill this pair specifically at slow tempo — your index doesn't move, but fingers 2/3/4 completely reorganize. The ear won't forgive a wrong shape even if the fret is right.
02
// PALM MUTING THE GROOVE
The original synth stab feel comes from controlled decay, not sustain. Apply light palm muting throughout the verse and intro — not a dead chug, but enough to shorten the ring. Lift the palm for the chorus to open up the sound. This dynamic shift between sections (muted verse / open chorus) replaces what the producer did with synth ADSR. You're the envelope now.
03
// THE FM / Bb INDEX LOCK
Fm and Bb both live at fret 1. Your index barre is already there — only fingers 2/3/4 change shape. In the chorus, Fm→Bb transitions are fast. Practice this isolated pair: barre fret 1, switch between Fm and Bb shapes with fingers 2/3/4 only. The index never leaves. This is the fastest transition in the song once you internalize it.
04
// HAND STAMINA PROTOCOL
At 117 BPM with all barres and a syncopated 8th pattern, this song will fatigue your fretting hand before any other in the set. Build to tempo gradually: 70 BPM → 88 BPM (75% gate) → 100 BPM → 117 BPM over multiple sessions. Never force through hand fatigue — you'll build bad tension habits. If you feel forearm tightness during a run, stop and shake out. Tight hands make bad barres.
08 // FEEL DESCRIPTION
// THE TARGET STATE
You're not playing rock. You're not playing disco. You're playing something that shouldn't exist — a metal arrangement that makes people want to dance. The groove is the thing. Every syncopated upstroke is a hook. The verse should feel like a coiled spring — tight, controlled, palm-muted, locked. The chorus releases the spring. The bridge breathes. Then the outro is the spring coiled again and released one final time into a single, devastating stop. If your bandmates aren't nodding their heads during your verse run-throughs, you're playing it too heavy. Back off the attack until the groove is more important than the weight.
