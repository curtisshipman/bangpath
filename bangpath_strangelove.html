01 // OVERVIEW THE BIG PICTURE
// Mission Brief

Depeche Mode's Strangelove is a synth-driven new wave track with a brooding, suspended quality built on the Em tonality. The original has almost no guitar — it's keyboards and drum machine throughout. For the !bang!path! metal arrangement, you're replacing that synth texture with strummed open-position chords that carry the harmonic movement while your drummer and bassist recreate the locked mechanical groove. The song is emotionally intense but sonically restrained — don't play it like a metal song. Play it like a song that happens to be heavy.

The chord set is all open position — no barres anywhere. After Into the Groove and Broken Wings, your fretting hand gets a holiday. The challenge here is not physical difficulty but the strumming pattern complexity and the 6/4 time signature in the bridge, which requires counting to 6 instead of 4. Both are addressable. Neither is insurmountable.

// ★ Good News — Easiest Barre Load in the Set

Em · C · Am · G · D · G/B — every single chord in this song is open position or a simple partial voicing. There are zero barre chords. G/B is the trickiest shape (partial G with B in bass) but it's manageable. If you've been grinding through the barre-heavy songs in this set, Strangelove is where your left hand exhales. Use that energy for the strumming pattern instead.

// ⚠ What 6/4 Means in Practice

In 6/4, each bar has 6 beats instead of 4. The feel is like a waltz that has been stretched — 1-2-3 / 4-5-6, with emphasis on beats 1 and 4. It doesn't mean the song gets faster or slower — the quarter note stays at 120 BPM — you just count to 6 instead of 4 before the next bar begins. The bridge feels longer because it is: each bar contains 50% more beats.

The safest approach in the bridge: count in 6, emphasize beat 1 and beat 4 with slightly stronger downstrokes. Let the drummer carry the time — your job is to stay locked with the kick drum pattern. If the bridge feels long, that's correct. Six beats is long. Trust your count.

The 6/4 bridge arrives three times. Each time it follows a 4/4 section. The shift isn't signalled melodically — you need to know the structure and feel it coming. Practice the transition specifically: count your last bar of 4/4 ("1-2-3-4") and land in 6/4 ("1-2-3-4-5-6") on the first bridge chord without stumbling. Your drummer should give a fill or cue at the transition. Agree on this cue before performance.

08 // TECHNIQUE NOTES
01
// LEARNING THE 16TH-NOTE PATTERN
The 2-bar primary pattern is the main challenge of this song. It runs in 16th notes — meaning 4 subdivisions per beat, 16 per bar, 32 across the 2-bar cycle. The arm must move in constant 16th-note motion; only some of those motions contact the strings. Start by air-strumming both bars with a metronome at 60 BPM. Mark which strokes contact strings with a tap on your leg. Build the muscle memory before adding the guitar. This takes days, not hours.
02
// G/B VOICING — THE AWKWARD CHORD
G/B is the only unusual chord in the song. The voicing: press fret 2 on the B-string with your index or middle finger, and let the D, G, B, and e strings ring open. Do not play the A or low E strings. The resulting sound is a D/G/B/e cluster with a B bass note — warm, unresolved, perfect for "worthwhile." Practice landing on it from the Am that precedes it in the verse. The transition is: release Am shape, keep index on B-string fret 2. That's the whole move.
03
// MANAGING THE 4/4 → 6/4 TRANSITION
The shift to 6/4 in the bridge is not announced — it's felt. The two strategies: (1) Know the structure so precisely that you anticipate it. (2) Follow your drummer's pattern shift — the kick/snare pattern changes at the bridge, and locking to the drummer is faster than counting independently. In rehearsal, explicitly mark the moment ("the bridge starts on the word 'There'll be times'") so the whole band has a shared cue. Never rely on just one person to hold the time signature — it's a band-wide coordination problem.
04
// DYNAMICS — THE NEW WAVE APPROACH
Strangelove is a brooding, atmospheric track. The original has enormous dynamic range — the verse is almost whispered, the chorus builds to full intensity. In the metal arrangement, preserve this architecture: verse = controlled, low attack, let the open chords breathe; chorus = full strum, full volume, intensity up; bridge = somewhere between — heavier than verse but more suspended than chorus. The outro decays. This dynamic shape is what makes the song feel like a performance rather than a run-through of chords.
09 // FEEL DESCRIPTION
// THE TARGET STATE
Strangelove is the song in this set where the new wave and metal meet most naturally. The brooding Em tonality, the mechanical groove, the emotional intensity — these are things both genres understand fluently. You're not translating this song from one language to another, you're playing it in a dialect it was already trying to speak. The 16th-note pattern creates a hypnotic, slightly obsessive quality that mirrors the original's synth texture. The bridge in 6/4 should feel like time is stretching — like the song is holding its breath. The chorus should feel like release. Play toward those feelings. The open Em at the end rings out. Let it.
